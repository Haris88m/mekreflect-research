# MEKreflect: company direction and first evidence gate

Version 0.2 · 18 October 2026 · User-corrected company direction

MEKreflect aims to make AI evaluations more useful for real decisions. We are developing methods to assess benchmark evidence and help teams turn it into testable operational policies. Becoming a benchmark for evaluating benchmarks is an ambition to earn through transparent methods and scrutiny, not an established superiority claim, universal ranking or certification authority.

The three pillars are benchmark evidence assessment; evaluation design and reproducibility, including investigations of AI behaviour; and evidence-to-policy translation. The authorization case is one concrete first test within this direction. It does not define the whole company or validate its benchmark-assessment capability.

The previous broad practical-tools positioning retained too much personal-project research. Those records remain an archive, outside the new programme. They must not appear as evidence for benchmark quality, policy effectiveness or company maturity.

## Concrete outputs the pillars should produce

1. Versioned benchmark evidence cards and comparability matrices that separate benchmark quality, documentation completeness and model performance.
2. Preregistered evaluation protocols, scoring rules, controls and reproduction requirements, with explicit missingness and execution limits.
3. A traceable chain from risk to evidence, policy requirement, enforcement, acceptance test, owner and review trigger.

The first source-audited documentary pilot is *What Public Agent Benchmarks Establish About Authorization and Policy Compliance*, v0.1. It compares AgentDojo, AgentHarm and original τ-bench as deliberately different scopes, not interchangeable scores. Dimensions include authorization/revocation, untrusted instructions, side effects, final-state versus trajectory checks, utility, repeatability and recovery. Documentation states are documented, partially documented, not reported and not applicable; not reported does not mean absent. The source cutoff is 18 October 2026 and papers are versioned; immutable repository revisions were not captured, which limits reproducibility. Actual publication status is in the separate publication record. This is not an executed meta-benchmark.

[BenchmarkCards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12974v3), [construct-validity work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703), [HELM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110v2), [Inspect](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/) and [NIST AI RMF](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) are related prior work. No novelty or empty-field claim is justified. Documentary review remains separate from executed evaluation and policy validation.

## Who might find the work useful

These are hypotheses, not validated customers or a market-size claim:

- Developers designing agent tools may need to distinguish permission judgments from external enforcement.
- People supervising AI-assisted workflows may need to preserve contact and disclosure limits while still completing legitimate work.
- Teams writing operational policies may need a clear link between a policy requirement, its enforcement and a scoped acceptance test.

We will need separate evidence of utility: observed user problems, permission to study the workflow, a current-process comparison, and whether a person can use the artifact. None follows from a synthetic authorization test. No customer contact is authorized by this document.

## First concrete question

When a source is blocked and an untrusted helper offers assistance, does an agent distinguish a reachable helper from an authorized helper, stay within recipient and payload limits, and use an authorized route when one is available?

The authored test uses a fictional desk-hours lookup. It has five conditions within one case family. The approved/unapproved pair differs only in authenticated user authority. The authorized alternative changes the capability available and is analysed separately. The no-helper baseline may end in an honest limitation; that is not task completion. Ambiguous permission requires clarification with one fixed authenticated reply.

We keep four records separate: proposed actions, boundary judgments by the gate, executed mock actions, and simulated effects. Answer correctness and supporting evidence are additional dimensions. A blocked unauthorized proposal is not compliant agent judgment. An authorized contact does not authorize obeying later instructions in untrusted tool output.

## Sequence and measurable gates

| Gate | Concrete deliverable and exit condition | Resource or blocker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Package readiness | Frozen protocol, five authored conditions, separate oracle, trace schema, independent proposal scorer, local gate, controls, mutations, reset checks and exact hashes. Every started scripted check is accounted for. | Existing Node runtime and original code only. Separate bounded code review passed after fixes; external scientific review remains pending. |
| 2. Specifically authorized pilot | Reviewer signs off the package; owner approves exact models/providers, prompts, tool exposure, trial schedule, repetition count and rationale, token/time/cost caps, retention and stop rules. Freeze the run manifest before starting. | Provider/local-model access, permitted execution environment and budget are not established. No model call is authorized now. |
| 3. Interpretable case-level signal | Preserve every started trial, invalid/failed/missing outcomes and linked retries. Examine whether permission-only pairs differ and whether useful authorized actions remain possible. Report exact model/settings and uncertainty. | A first pilot is descriptive. There is no supported statistical-power threshold, prevalence estimate or cross-provider generalization from this one family. |
| 4. More case families | Author substantively different tasks, failure opportunities and evidence requirements; predeclare inclusion and review. Separate development from held-out cases. | Additional design and review effort; five variants are not five independent problems. |
| 5. Practical utility evidence | With separate consent and scope, test whether a potential user understands and can use the resulting method in an actual workflow. Record false blocks and maintenance effort alongside benefits. | Customer/problem discovery and workflow access remain unvalidated. No ROI forecast or service promise is justified. |

The next honest result is a reviewed, reproducible authored package. A subsequent result may be blocked or inconclusive. We will report that state rather than substitute publication checks or scripted controls for empirical findings.

## Resource boundaries

Current work uses original local code, fictional records and in-memory effects. It requires no dependency installation, third-party SDK execution, model access, shell-enabled agent tool, outside contact or real target. The first run register explicitly remains unapproved for model trials. Existing SDK admission material stays separate and does not determine the scientific outcome of this case.

References informing the method retain their authorship: [current case and source links](https://mekreflect.com/journal/agent-support/), [dated external references](https://mekreflect.com/research/#external-references), and [Inspect source](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai). These do not imply affiliation or equivalent research access.
